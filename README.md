# DealFlow

DealFlow is a brand deal CRM for solo creators.

**Every sponsorship, tracked from first email to final payment, in one place.**

Solo creators are running real businesses, but most of their brand partnership workflow still lives across spreadsheets, inboxes, DMs, calendar reminders, notes apps, invoice templates, and memory. DealFlow turns that scattered process into a focused operating system for managing sponsorship revenue.

## The Pain Point

Brand deals are one of the most important income streams for creators, but the workflow around them is still painfully manual.

A creator might receive an inbound sponsorship request in Gmail, negotiate terms in Instagram DMs, save the brand contact in a spreadsheet, track the deliverables in Notion, set a calendar reminder for the publish date, create the invoice in a separate tool, and then remember to follow up when the payment is late. None of those tools are wrong on their own, but together they create a fragile system for work that can be worth hundreds or thousands of dollars per deal.

The result is predictable:

- Deals get buried in inboxes and direct messages.
- Content deadlines are missed or remembered too late.
- Payment follow-ups depend on memory instead of a system.
- Creators undercharge because they cannot easily see past rates.
- Brand contacts are lost when a PR rep changes roles.
- Invoice creation becomes repetitive admin work.
- Earnings are hard to understand until tax season or a crisis.

For creators managing 5 to 15 active sponsorship conversations at once, this is not a small inconvenience. It directly affects cash flow, professionalism, and the amount of time they can spend creating.

## Why Existing Tools Fall Short

Most tools around the creator economy are built for brands, agencies, or marketplaces. They help companies discover creators, manage campaigns, and measure influencer performance. The creator is usually the supply side of the platform, not the customer being served.

Solo creators usually fall back to general-purpose tools:

- **Google Sheets** gives flexibility, but it does not remind the creator when payment is overdue, generate invoices, or show a real sponsorship pipeline.
- **Notion templates** are nicer than spreadsheets, but they are still passive databases. They do not automate the parts of the workflow that cause missed money.
- **Email and DMs** are where deals begin, but they are poor systems of record once there are multiple brands, deadlines, files, and payments involved.
- **Enterprise influencer platforms** are expensive, agency-oriented, and built around campaign management from the brand's point of view.

DealFlow is built from the creator's side of the table.

## The Solution

DealFlow gives solo creators a single workspace for managing sponsorships from the first conversation through final payment.

Instead of asking creators to maintain a spreadsheet and remember the rest, DealFlow organizes each deal around the actual lifecycle of a paid brand collaboration: lead, negotiation, contract, deliverables, approval, publishing, invoice, and payment.

The product is designed around a simple promise:

**A creator should always know what deals are active, what is due next, who owes money, and what they should charge next time.**

## What DealFlow Does

### 1. Tracks Every Deal In A Visual Pipeline

Creators can see each sponsorship as a card moving through a clear pipeline. The board shows where every brand deal stands, which opportunities are still early, which campaigns are active, and which deals are complete.

This replaces the vague question of "What am I working on right now?" with a concrete view of the creator's sponsorship business.

### 2. Centralizes Brand And Contact Details

Every brand, contact, email, campaign, note, and collaboration history can live in one place. When a creator works with a brand again, they can see what happened last time, what rate was agreed, who handled the conversation, and whether the relationship is worth continuing.

This matters because sponsorships are relationship-driven. Losing context means losing leverage.

### 3. Turns Deadlines Into Reminders

Brand campaigns have multiple deadlines: content drafts, approval windows, publish dates, exclusivity dates, invoice dates, and payment due dates. DealFlow treats those dates as part of the deal, not as separate calendar chores.

The goal is to make missed deadlines and forgotten payment follow-ups much less likely.

### 4. Generates Invoices From Deal Data

Creators should not have to recreate the same invoice details manually after every campaign. DealFlow can use the information already attached to a deal to help generate a professional invoice, track its status, and mark payment when it arrives.

This reduces repetitive admin and gives creators better visibility into outstanding revenue.

### 5. Shows Earnings And Outstanding Payments

DealFlow is not just about tracking work. It is about understanding creator income.

The earnings dashboard is intended to show monthly revenue, outstanding invoices, overdue payments, pipeline value, average deal value, and rate history. This helps creators make better decisions about pricing, follow-ups, and which brands are worth their time.

### 6. Helps Creators Communicate Professionally

Creators repeatedly send the same kinds of messages: outreach, rate negotiation, contract confirmation, content submission, invoice follow-up, and campaign wrap-up. DealFlow includes reusable email templates so creators can respond faster without sounding unprofessional or starting from scratch every time.

## Core User Journey

1. A creator signs up and completes a lightweight onboarding flow.
2. They add their first brand deal with the brand name, contact, platforms, deliverables, rate, and key dates.
3. The deal appears on the pipeline board in the correct stage.
4. As the sponsorship progresses, the creator moves the deal through negotiation, contract signed, content creation, approval, live, invoice sent, and paid.
5. DealFlow keeps the important context attached to the deal: notes, contacts, files, tasks, deliverables, invoice details, and activity history.
6. The creator receives reminders before important content and payment deadlines.
7. When the campaign is complete, the creator generates an invoice from the deal data.
8. Once payment arrives, the creator marks it paid and the earnings dashboard updates.
9. The next time a similar brand reaches out, the creator can check past rates and negotiate with more confidence.

## MVP Scope

DealFlow is being built as a focused MVP. The goal is not to build a huge creator platform immediately. The goal is to solve the highest-value admin problems first.

### Included In The MVP

- Deal pipeline board
- Deal detail views
- Brand and contact management
- Task and deadline tracking
- Automated reminder foundation
- Invoice generation and payment status tracking
- Earnings and analytics dashboard
- Rate history
- Email templates for common creator-brand communication
- Stripe subscription support
- Mobile-responsive web experience

### Intentionally Out Of Scope For The MVP

- Native mobile app
- AI contract review
- Media kit builder
- Public creator marketplace
- Brand discovery tools
- Manager/team accounts
- Chrome extension
- Bank account integrations

Those features may become useful later, but the first version stays focused on the workflow creators already struggle with every week.

## Who It Is For

DealFlow is built for solo creators who are already earning from brand partnerships or are starting to manage multiple sponsorship conversations.

The primary users are:

- Micro creators with roughly 10K to 100K followers
- Mid-tier creators with roughly 100K to 500K followers
- YouTubers, Instagram creators, TikTok creators, podcasters, newsletter writers, and niche experts
- Creators managing sponsorships without a full-time manager or agency team

These users do not need an enterprise influencer platform. They need a lightweight business tool that respects how solo creators actually work.

## Market Opportunity

The creator economy is large, but creator business tooling is still uneven. Many tools help brands find creators, schedule social posts, or manage paid campaigns from the buyer side. Far fewer tools help creators manage the business operations behind their own sponsorship income.

Brand partnerships can represent a major share of a creator's revenue. For a solo creator, missing one invoice follow-up or undercharging one campaign can cost more than months of software subscription fees. This makes the problem both frequent and financially meaningful.

DealFlow is positioned around that gap: a creator-first CRM for sponsorship operations, priced for individuals rather than agencies.

## Product Principles

- **Creator-first:** The product is designed for the person selling the sponsorship, not the brand buying it.
- **Pipeline clarity:** Creators should be able to understand their sponsorship business at a glance.
- **Less manual admin:** Repeated work like invoices, follow-ups, and templates should be streamlined.
- **Financial visibility:** Outstanding payments, overdue invoices, and rate history should be easy to see.
- **Mobile-friendly web first:** Creators often work from their phones, but the MVP avoids the complexity of a native app.
- **Focused MVP:** The product should solve the core pain before expanding into broader creator tools.

## Tech Stack

DealFlow is a modern web application built with:

- **Next.js** for the application framework
- **React** for the UI
- **Tailwind CSS** for styling
- **Prisma** for database modeling and access
- **PostgreSQL** as the primary database
- **Supabase** direction for auth, storage, and database infrastructure
- **Stripe** for subscriptions and billing
- **Resend** and React Email for transactional email
- **Recharts** for dashboard visualizations

## Local PostgreSQL And Adminer

This project includes a local Docker setup for PostgreSQL and Adminer.

Add the local database settings to `.env.local`:

```bash
POSTGRES_USER=postgres
POSTGRES_PASSWORD=postgres
POSTGRES_DB=postgres
POSTGRES_PORT=5432
ADMINER_PORT=8080
DATABASE_URL="postgresql://postgres:postgres@localhost:5432/postgres"
```

Start the services:

```bash
docker compose up -d
```

Open Adminer at `http://localhost:8080`.

Use these values:

- System: `PostgreSQL`
- Server: `postgres`
- Username: value of `POSTGRES_USER`
- Password: value of `POSTGRES_PASSWORD`
- Database: value of `POSTGRES_DB`

Stop the services:

```bash
docker compose down
```

Reset local database data:

```bash
docker compose down -v
```
